## 🎯 How To Read This Calendar

Every level README ships its own weekly rhythm built around **three or four short sittings a week**. This file is the alternative: the same content re-cut into **exactly two longer sessions a week**, which is what most parents, tutors and after-school clubs can actually run.

Both are correct. Pick one and stay with it.

| | Level README rhythm | This pacing guide |
|---|---|---|
| **Sessions/week** | 3–4 | 2 |
| **Session length** | 35–60 min | 60–90 min |
| **Weekly hours** | 2.5–2.8 | 2.0–3.0 |
| **Best for** | A learner working alone on their own schedule | A guided pair with fixed slots (e.g. Tue + Sat) |
| **Total weeks** | 12 / 20 / 24 / 28 | 12 / 20 / 24 / 28 — identical |

**Column meanings in every table below:**

| Column | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **Wk** | Week number within the level |
| **Session A** | The first session of the week — usually 🪝 Hook + 🧠 Concept + 🔍 Worked Example |
| **Session B** | The second session — usually 💻 Hands-On + ✍️ Practice + 🛠️ Mini-Project |
| **Deliverable** | The thing that must exist by the end of the week. Tick it in the [progress tracker](progress-tracker.md). |

> 🔑 **The rule that outranks the calendar.** A week is done when the deliverable exists, not when Sunday arrives. Slipping a week is normal and costs nothing. Carrying a gap forward costs everything, because this course has no forward references — every module depends only on modules before it, which means every hole gets heavier.

> ⚠️ **Where the arithmetic is tight.** 168 sessions × 75 min = 210 hours against a 216-hour course. Levels 1–3 have comfortable slack. **Level 4 does not** — its 56 sessions cover ~79 hours, which means Level 4 sessions genuinely run at the 90-minute end, and the capstone weeks (23–28) will spill into extra time. Plan for that rather than discovering it in week 26.

### Calendar placement across school years

Real families need to know when the holidays land. Here is one workable mapping (Northern-Hemisphere school year, ~36 teaching weeks/year):

| School year | Grade | What runs | Weeks used |
|---|:--:|---|:--:|
| **Year 1** | 6 | Level 1 (12 wk) → break → Level 2 weeks 1–14 | 26 of 36 |
| **Year 2** | 7 | Level 2 weeks 15–20 → Level 3 weeks 1–20 | 26 of 36 |
| **Year 3** | 8–9 | Level 3 weeks 21–24 → Level 4 weeks 1–22 | 26 of 36 |
| **Year 4** | 9–10 | Level 4 weeks 23–28 + the capstone tail | 8+ of 36 |

That leaves ~10 spare weeks a year for exam season, illness, holidays and the weeks where nothing works. **Do not plan those away.** A calendar with zero slack fails in week 9 of 84.

If the learner starts in grade 6 and runs at this pace, they finish Level 4 around grade 9–10 — two years earlier than the grade bands suggest. That is fine, and it is a good argument for spending the extra time on [Stretch] exercises rather than on speed.

### Level 1 pacing rules

1. **Weeks 5 and 6 must not merge.** Module 5 ends in pride at 91%. Module 6 exists to show that number may have been a lie. The gap needs days of pride in between or the lesson doesn't bite. This is the single most-violated rule in the level.
2. **Week 10 is not slack.** It is where nine weeks of activities become one idea. Skipping it means the capstone gets built by someone who did nine things rather than someone who learned one.
3. **Session B always ends with a made thing.** If you run out of time, cut the Practice exercises, not the build.
4. **60 minutes is a ceiling, not a target.** If attention is gone at 40 minutes, stop at 40. Two good 40s beat one bad 80.

### Level 1 variations

| If you have… | Do this |
|---|---|
| **One 90-min session/week** | 18 weeks. Alternate: concept week, project week. Keep M5 and M6 in different weeks regardless. |
| **Two 45-min sessions/week** | Stay at 12 weeks but add a third short sitting for the mini-project in weeks 5, 7, 8 and 9 — those four builds are the long ones. |
| **A school class** | Weeks 1–9 as lessons, week 10 as the assessment, 11–12 as a class **AI Fair** where everyone presents a booth on one afternoon. |
| **A learner who reads slowly** | Read alternate paragraphs aloud together. It halves the perceived load and gives natural pause points. Add 4 weeks. |

### Level 2 pacing rules

1. **Type the code. Never paste it.** Costs about 20 extra minutes a week and is the highest-return 20 minutes in the level. Fingers have to learn where the colons go.
2. **Weeks 5 and 10 are the checkpoint weeks and they are not slack.** They convert "things I followed" into "things I can do." A learner who skips week 5 arrives at Module 6 unable to write a `for` loop without looking one up.
3. **Predict before you run, every single time.** Write the expected output down first. This is Session B's core discipline.
4. **Weeks 12 and 14 must not merge.** Module 9 only bites if Module 8's high training score has had time to feel like a victory.

|---|---|

| **A school class** | Weeks 1–14 as lessons, 15 as the assessment, 16–20 as a project block ending in a Data Detective showcase. |

| **A learner struggling with M5–M6** | Add 2 weeks. numpy shapes and pandas fluency are the two things Level 3 assumes most aggressively; a gap here is expensive later. |

### Level 3 pacing rules

1. **Pen before terminal, every single time.** Modules 4, 5, 7 and 9 each have a worked example meant to be done by hand *first*. The verification moment — "my number was 0.802 and the code says 0.8022" — is the actual learning event. Run the code first and you get the answer while losing the lesson.
2. **Never skip weeks 7 and 12.** Week 7 turns the pipeline from a thing followed into a thing built. Week 12 turns backprop from five copied rules into five rederivable ones. Skip week 12 and autograd becomes magic again in week 13.
3. **When the loss doesn't go down, change one thing.** Write down what you changed and what happened. That habit is what the journal is for and it is what this level is secretly teaching.
4. **Never skip the gradient check.** It is the only thing standing between the learner and hours spent debugging a network that was never going to learn.
5. **Weeks 8–11 are the hardest four weeks in the course.** See [Cliff 3](README.md#cliff-3--level-3-modules-45-the-maths-wall). Budget the spare time here, not in Module 8.

### Level 3 variations

| If you have… | Do this |
|---|---|
| **~1.5 h/week** | 32 weeks. Give M4, M5, M6 and M7 three weeks each. Never drop a mini-project — the capstone ships one. |
| **~6 h/week (a holiday)** | 12 weeks, one module a week — but keep **M4 and M5 at least three days apart**. |
| **A school class** | Weeks 1–19 as lessons, 20 as the assessment, 21–24 as a project block ending in a **demo day** where each learner runs their HTTP service live. |
| **A learner who already knows sklearn** | Sit assessment Q1–Q7 and debug problem D1 **first**. 100% earns compressing M1–M3 into three weeks of mini-projects. You may **never** compress M4–M5 on that basis; they are the point of the level. |
| **A GPU or a fast laptop** | Don't rush M6–M7 because the epochs finish quickly. The time in those modules is thinking about shapes, not waiting. Spend the saved hours on M7's per-class recall extension. |
| **A learner drowning in M4** | Insert a 1-week detour: slope of a straight line on paper, then slope of a curve at a point, then "the gradient points uphill and you go the other way," with no code at all. Then resume. |

### Level 4 pacing rules

1. **Pen before terminal in Modules 1–4.** Same rule as Level 3, and it matters more here, not less.
2. **Offline stubs before the API in Modules 5–9.** Every API module has a free offline path. Your bugs live in parsing, scoring and reporting — none of which needs the model. Get the harness right for $0.00, *then* point it at Claude.
3. **Never skip weeks 10 and 17.** Week 10 is where attention stops being copied and starts being writable. Week 17 is where RAG and the agent loop become one picture instead of two folders.
4. **Freeze the eval set before you build the thing it measures.** Weeks 11, 18 and 24 all enforce this. A test set edited after seeing your score is not a test set; it is a mirror.
5. **Keep weeks 4 and 5 apart.** Module 2's vanishing-gradient measurement is the setup; Module 3's attention is the punchline. The joke does not work rushed.
6. **If you fall behind, drop [Stretch] and "level it up" — never a mini-project, and never Module 9.** Modules 6, 7 and 8's outputs *are* the capstone's raw material.

## 🧯 When The Calendar Breaks

It will. Here's the triage.

| Situation | Do this | Do NOT do this |
|---|---|---|
| **Two weeks missed (illness, exams)** | Restart with a retrieval session: rebuild the last mini-project from a blank file. Then continue. | Skip a module to "catch up" |
| **A month missed** | Go back one module and redo its mini-project, then continue. | Resume where you stopped |
| **A whole term missed** | Run the level's [exit checks](../CURRICULUM_MAP.md#-level-exit-checks) for the *previous* level. Whatever fails is your restart point. | Assume it's all still in there |
| **Consistently 30 min short each session** | Move to the level README's 3–4 short sittings rhythm instead of 2 long ones. | Cut the mini-project every week |
| **Motivation gone, not comprehension** | Jump to the level's capstone brief and read it out loud. Then ask what they'd need to build it. Nothing restores motivation like seeing the destination. | Push harder on the schedule |
| **Comprehension gone, not motivation** | Drop one level down for two weeks on the specific idea. The [spiral table](../CURRICULUM_MAP.md#-the-spiral-progression) tells you exactly where the earlier pass lives. | Reread the same module louder |
| **Behind by 6+ weeks and it's stressful** | Formally re-plan. Write the new end date down and say it out loud together. Anxiety comes from a silently-missed target, not a moved one. | Pretend the original date is alive |

## 🔑 Key Takeaways

- **84 weeks, 168 sessions, ~210 contact hours.** Levels 1–3 have slack; Level 4 does not.
- **The deliverable ends the week, not the date.** Slipping is free; carrying a gap forward is not.
- **Checkpoint weeks (L2 5 & 10, L3 7 & 12, L4 10 & 17) look like slack and are the opposite.** They are where "followed" becomes "can do."
- **Four pairs must never be merged:** L1 M5→M6, L2 M8→M9, L3 M4→M5, L4 M2→M3. Each is a setup and a punchline that needs days in between.
- **Pen before terminal** from Level 3 onward; **offline stubs before the API** from Level 4 Module 5 onward.
- **The summer intensive works, and it costs retention.** The hybrid — one level per summer, 90 min/week of term-time consolidation — is the version we'd choose.
- **When the calendar breaks, diagnose with the exit checks, not with the date.**
