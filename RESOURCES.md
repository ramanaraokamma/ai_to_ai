## 🪝 Read This First

Three honest warnings before the lists.

### ⚠️ Verify every link before you hand it to a learner

```
   ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  THE LINK-ROT RULE                                                    │
   │                                                                       │
   │  Everything here was real and free at the time of writing.            │
   │  The web moves. Sites go paid, courses get retired, channels stop.    │
   │                                                                       │
   │  BEFORE you point a learner at anything on this page:                 │
   │    1. Open it yourself.                                               │
   │    2. Check it's still free and still has no surprise account wall.   │
   │    3. Check the content is what the description says.                 │
   │                                                                       │
   │  Sixty seconds. Do it every time.                                     │
   └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Where a resource is time-sensitive or has changed hands before, it's marked **⏳ check first**. Where a resource has known age or account requirements, it's marked **🔞 account/age check**.

### ⚠️ Nothing on this page is required

Every module in this course is self-contained. Every dataset used is either typed out in the file, generated by the code, or built into `sklearn.datasets` / `torchvision`. **You never need to download anything from this page to finish a module.**

These are for the learner who finishes a module and wants more, or the guide who wants a second explanation in a different voice.

> 🔑 **The trap this page creates.** A learner who watches ten videos about backprop has not learned backprop. A learner who wrote it in NumPy and gradient-checked it has. **External resources are for a second pass, never a first one.** If a learner is reaching for a video instead of a pen, that's avoidance wearing a hat.

### ⚠️ Difficulty is not the same as level

Many of the best free resources are pitched at university students. A Level 2 learner *can* watch a Stanford lecture; they will absorb about 4% of it and conclude they're not smart enough. Each entry below has a **fit** note. Trust it over your instinct that "they'll pick it up."

**Grades 11–12 · transformers + LLMs · the goal is the frontier, and the discipline to attack your own work**

## 📄 Primary Sources (papers a Level 4 learner can genuinely read)

Reading a paper is a skill and these are the right ones to learn it on. All free on arXiv or the publisher's site.

| Paper | Why this one | Read after |
|---|---|---|
| **"Attention Is All You Need"** (Vaswani et al., 2017) | The transformer. You will have implemented it before reading it, which is the correct order and makes it readable. | Module 3 |
| **"Language Models are Few-Shot Learners"** (GPT-3, Brown et al., 2020) | Where in-context learning and scaling came from | Module 4 |
| **"Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback"** (InstructGPT, Ouyang et al., 2022) | The clearest description of SFT → reward model → RLHF | Module 4 |
| **"Direct Preference Optimization"** (Rafailov et al., 2023) | DPO, and why it replaced a lot of RLHF plumbing | Module 4 |
| **"LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models"** (Hu et al., 2021) | Fifteen lines of code, one clean idea | Module 8 |
| **"Retrieval-Augmented Generation..."** (Lewis et al., 2020) | The original RAG framing | Module 6 |
| **"Model Cards for Model Reporting"** (Mitchell et al., 2019) | The document Level 3's capstone asks you to write | Level 3 capstone |
| **"Datasheets for Datasets"** (Gebru et al., 2018) | The data card, formalized | Level 1 M2, revisited in Level 3 |
| **"On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots"** (Bender et al., 2021) | The best-known critical paper on scale. Read it *and* a rebuttal. | Module 9 |

> 🔑 **How to read a paper at this level.** Abstract → figures → conclusion → then the method section, and only then the rest. Karpathy's and Kilcher's walkthroughs are good training wheels. The real skill — reading a paper's *claims* against its *evidence* — takes about thirty papers, so start counting.

## ⚠️ Common Resource Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Watching the video instead of writing the code** | Videos feel like progress and cost no effort | Rule: **the module first, the video second.** Karpathy after your GPT, 3Blue1Brown after your backprop. |
| **Handing a Level 2 learner a Stanford lecture** | It's free and it's about the right topic | Check the fit column. A learner who understands 4% of a lecture concludes they're the problem. |
| **Chasing a Kaggle leaderboard** | Numbers going up is addictive | Submit a dumb baseline first. Never submit a model you can't explain. Score the [project rubric](teacher-guide/rubrics.md), not the leaderboard. |
| **Downloading a 3 GB dataset for a mini-project** | Bigger feels more real | Every module's data is built in for a reason. Big data teaches waiting, not modelling. |
| **Trusting a tutorial over the official docs** | Tutorials are friendlier | ⏳ Especially in Level 4. API parameters change; blog posts don't. The docs are the truth. |
| **Reading a paper before implementing the thing** | It seems like the scholarly order | It's backwards. "Attention Is All You Need" is readable *because* you already wrote attention. |
| **Signing a young learner up for accounts everywhere** | Each one seems small | 🔞 Every account is a data-sharing decision. Level 1 Module 9 makes this a lesson — apply it to yourselves first. |
| **Treating this page as a to-do list** | It's a list, and lists want completing | It's a menu. A learner who finishes the 36 modules and touches nothing here has completed the course properly. |

## 🔑 Key Takeaways

- **Nothing on this page is required.** Every module is self-contained; every dataset is generated, typed out, or built in.
- **Verify every link before handing it over.** Sixty seconds, every time. Watch for ⏳ and 🔞 markers.
- **Second pass, never first.** Videos and articles confirm what you built. They cannot replace building it.
- **Fit beats topic.** A resource on the right subject at the wrong level teaches a learner that they're not smart enough.
- **The best dataset is usually the learner's own** — their week in Level 1, their question in Level 2, their journal in Level 4.
- **Free and excellent exists at every level:** Automate the Boring Stuff, ISL, Neural Networks and Deep Learning, Understanding Deep Learning, the scikit-learn user guide, 3Blue1Brown, StatQuest, Karpathy, the Anthropic docs.
- **Publishing beats placing.** A public, documented, honestly-evaluated project is worth more than a leaderboard rank in every conversation that matters.
