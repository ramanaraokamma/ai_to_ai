### 2. 🔢 The maths you need — taught to you first

**One idea: low-rank.** You need it as counting, and the counting is three steps. Do them on paper before class.

**(a) A grid from a column and a row.** Take a column of four numbers `(1, 2, 0, -1)` and a row of four `(2, 1, 0, 3)`. Multiply every column entry by every row entry and write the results in a 4 × 4 grid (Block P7 prints it). Sixteen cells, built from `4 + 4 = 8` numbers. **Every row of the grid is a multiple of the row you started with**: row 1 is `1 x (2,1,0,3)`, row 2 is `2 x` it, row 3 is `0 x` it, row 4 is `-1 x` it. A grid that can be built this way is called **rank 1**. Change *one* cell (Grid Card B) and the rows are no longer multiples of one row; you now need a second column-and-row pair, so it is **rank 2**. "Rank" is just **how many column-times-row layers you need to build the grid**. That is the whole definition the student gets. Do not say "linearly independent", "span" or "singular value"; the teacher needs none of them.

**(b) The count.** A projection that takes `in` numbers to `out` numbers stores a grid of `in x out` cells. A rank-`r` **patch** stores two thin grids: `A` of shape `r x in` and `B` of shape `out x r`, and uses their product `B` times `A` (an `out x in` grid) as a correction. Count: `r x in + out x r`. For our `64 x 64` projection at `r = 4`: `4 x 64 + 64 x 4 = 256 + 256 = 512` against `64 x 64 = 4,096`. A share of `512 / 4096 = 0.125`. **The saving is not free**: a rank-4 patch can only make corrections that are sums of four column-times-row layers. The patch is as large as the whole projection at `r = 32` (`2 x 32 x 64 = 4,096`) and **larger** beyond it (`r = 64` is twice the size; K1).

**(c) The percentage, for the whole model.** Count trainable numbers and divide by all numbers. Ours: each of the two Blocks has a `q` and a `v` projection, so **four** patches of `512` = `2,048`; the five-way head has `64 x 5 + 5 = 325`; trainable `2,373`. The frozen encoder has `121,152`; total `121,152 + 325 + 2,048 = 123,525`. So `2,373 / 123,525 = 1.92%` (the adapters alone are `1.66%`). This is the number the README asked for; it is the module's "1.11%" recomputed for *our* model. **A LoRA percentage belongs to one model and one choice of `r` and of target layers.**

**One number you will be asked: "why is `B` zero and `A` random?"** The patch adds `B` times `A` (times a scale). If `B` is zero the patch adds nothing: step 0 is the base model. If `A` were zero too, then *the gradient of each depends on the other*, so both gradients are zero forever and nothing ever moves (K5 proves it: `0.0 0.0` after 100 steps). With `B = 0` and `A` small-random, the very first gradient reaches `B` (printed: `biggest |B.grad| = 0.00208`) and not yet `A` (`biggest |A.grad| = 0.0`), and from step 1 both move.

### 4. The constructs — four new, the rest old

- **`nn.Parameter(tensor)`**: a tensor PyTorch will train. Anything you assign to a module as an `nn.Parameter` shows up in `model.parameters()`; a plain tensor does not. `self.A` and `self.B` are the two in `LoRALinear`.
- **`module.requires_grad_(False)`**: freezes **every** parameter inside the module, in place; the trailing underscore means "changes it, returns nothing new". `model.enc.requires_grad_(False)` freezes the whole encoder; `model.requires_grad_(True)` undoes it. Point out that `requires_grad=True` on a tensor (Week 2) is the same switch, one tensor at a time.
- **`nn.init.zeros_(t)` and `nn.init.normal_(t, std=0.02)`**: fill an existing tensor in place with zeros or small random numbers. Again the underscore. `std` is the typical size (Week 3's "typical size").
- **`copy.deepcopy(model)`**: a complete, independent copy of a model. Week 5 copied a dictionary of weights; today copies the whole thing. Without it, `lora` and `base` are two names for one model and the comparison is meaningless (Clinic D4).
- **Old and used freely**: the Week 16 Block (`q`, `k`, `v`, `proj`, `masked_fill`) — now with `pad.view(B, 1, 1, T)` and no causal mask, so **every word sees every word** (that is what makes it an *encoder*; Week 17's GPT is a *decoder*); `F.cross_entropy(..., ignore_index=-100)` (Week 12, used for the hidden words); `nn.ModuleList`; `.T`; `@`; a list comprehension `[p for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad]` (Level 2, used inside `fit`); `torch.randperm`; `time.time()`; Week 30's `score` and `compare`.

### 6. The honest limits of today

- **One ticket is 0.033.** Every difference between `18`, `19` and `20` out of 30 is one or two tickets. The six-seed spread for a single method is 1 to 3 tickets (base 15 to 18, full 18 to 20, LoRA 18 to 19). Nothing in this week ranks full fine-tuning against LoRA.
- **The eval was used to look, not to tune, with one admission.** Steps (100), learning rates (`3e-3` head and LoRA, `3e-4` full) and `r = 4` are the first values tried and were never changed after seeing a score; the rank sweep (K3) is reported, not chosen from. **But the author saw eval scores while building the pretraining text**: a first version left about 30% of the eval words unknown and its vocabulary was widened (the eval sentences themselves were never copied in; the scan enforces that). Tell the student this; it is the same sin at small size. If a student starts changing `lr` until billing recovers, that is tuning on the test set — stop them and point at Week 30's frozen eval.
- **The pretraining text knows the eval vocabulary.** Said in section 3. Not fixable in this week.
- **`out_of_scope` has 8 training tickets against 14 elsewhere.** The README's planned story is that under-represented classes are the ones that suffer. In our runs it is *billing* that falls (three seeds of six) and `out_of_scope` falls in one. Do not teach the planned story; teach the measured one, and let the student propose why (billing and refund both mention money: `'took the money twice on the 3rd'` is predicted `technical` after LoRA, K2).
- **Not measured**: any real encoder, any speed advantage (full `0.24 s`, LoRA `0.21 s` for 100 steps here: no difference at this size), any memory saving (we compute the optimiser-state arithmetic in K1 Q5 but do not measure it).

### 8. How deep to go, and where to stop

Stop at: *"a patch of two thin grids, starting at zero, beside frozen weights; count the numbers; check step 0; read the table by category."* Do **not** derive why low-rank corrections are enough in real fine-tuning (it is an empirical finding, and we did not test it). Do **not** go into alpha tuning, QLoRA, merging adapters, or adapters on other layers; if asked, say "same idea, different places to attach it". If a student asks why `alpha / r`: *"it is a volume knob on the patch; with `alpha = 2r` the knob is always 2."* If they ask whether LoRA is better than full fine-tuning: *"on 30 tickets we cannot tell; on the real thing people report it is close; I have not measured it."* If a student asks about overfitting: both final training losses are near zero (`0.014`, `0.005`) on 64 tickets while eval is `0.633`; train accuracy of 100% against 63% is what Week 5 called memorising, and it is why Week 30's eval was frozen *first*.

```python
# p4_encoder.py - Week 31 block P4: YOUR Week 16 Block with the causal mask replaced by a padding mask, and an Encoder of two.
import copy
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F


class Block(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, d, H):
        super().__init__()
        self.H = H
        self.ln1 = nn.LayerNorm(d)
        self.q = nn.Linear(d, d, bias=False)
        self.k = nn.Linear(d, d, bias=False)
        self.v = nn.Linear(d, d, bias=False)
        self.proj = nn.Linear(d, d)
        self.ln2 = nn.LayerNorm(d)
        self.up = nn.Linear(d, 4 * d)
        self.act = nn.GELU()
        self.down = nn.Linear(4 * d, d)

    def forward(self, x, pad):                              # pad: (B, T), True where the slot is padding
        B, T, d = x.shape
        dh = d // self.H
        h = self.ln1(x)
        q = self.q(h).view(B, T, self.H, dh).transpose(1, 2)
        k = self.k(h).view(B, T, self.H, dh).transpose(1, 2)
        v = self.v(h).view(B, T, self.H, dh).transpose(1, 2)
        scores = q @ k.transpose(-2, -1) / dh ** 0.5
        scores = scores.masked_fill(pad.view(B, 1, 1, T), float("-inf"))     # nobody looks at padding
        mixed = (F.softmax(scores, dim=-1) @ v).transpose(1, 2).reshape(B, T, d)
        x = x + self.proj(mixed)
        return x + self.down(self.act(self.up(self.ln2(x))))


class Encoder(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, V, d=64, H=4, L=2, T=16):
        super().__init__()
        self.d = d
        self.tok = nn.Embedding(V, d)
        self.pos = nn.Embedding(T, d)
        self.blocks = nn.ModuleList([Block(d, H) for _ in range(L)])
        self.ln_f = nn.LayerNorm(d)

    def forward(self, ids):                                 # ids: (B, T) -> (B, T, d); every word sees every word
        pad = ids == 0
        x = self.tok(ids) + self.pos(torch.arange(ids.shape[1]))
        for blk in self.blocks:
            x = blk(x, pad)
        return self.ln_f(x)


def count(model):
    return sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters())


torch.manual_seed(0)
encoder = Encoder(V)
print("encoder parameters:", count(encoder))
print("one Block:", count(encoder.blocks[0]), " of which q and v:", count(encoder.blocks[0].q) + count(encoder.blocks[0].v))
print("output shape:", tuple(encoder(X_train[:3]).shape))
```

```python
# p6_classifier.py - Week 31 block P6: a classifier on top of the pretrained encoder, and the three helpers used all week.
X_eval = torch.tensor([encode(t) for t, _ in EVAL])


class Clf(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, enc, n=5):
        super().__init__()
        self.enc = enc
        self.head = nn.Linear(enc.d, n)                      # d numbers -> one score per label

    def forward(self, ids):
        h = self.enc(ids)                                    # (B, T, d)
        keep = (ids != 0).unsqueeze(-1).float()              # 1.0 for real words, 0.0 for padding
        return self.head((h * keep).sum(1) / keep.sum(1))    # average the real words, then score
```

```python
# p8_lora.py - Week 31 block P8: LoRA. Keep the old projection frozen; add a thin-times-thin patch beside it.
class LoRALinear(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, base, r=4, alpha=8):
        super().__init__()
        self.base = base
        self.base.requires_grad_(False)                          # freeze the old weights in place
        self.A = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(r, base.in_features))  # a tensor PyTorch will train
        self.B = nn.Parameter(torch.zeros(base.out_features, r))
        nn.init.normal_(self.A, std=0.02)                        # A starts small and random
        nn.init.zeros_(self.B)                                   # B starts at exactly zero, so the patch adds nothing yet
        self.scale = alpha / r                                   # a volume knob on the patch

    def forward(self, x):
        return self.base(x) + (x @ self.A.T @ self.B.T) * self.scale


def add_lora(model, r=4, alpha=8):
    model.enc.requires_grad_(False)                              # freeze the whole encoder first
    for blk in model.enc.blocks:
        blk.q = LoRALinear(blk.q, r, alpha)                      # the new modules are trainable; the head still is too
        blk.v = LoRALinear(blk.v, r, alpha)
    return model


def trainable(model):
    return sum(p.numel() for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad)
```

## 🐞 The Debugging Clinic

### How to teach debugging without giving the answer

Run each block; ask *"what did you expect to see, and what did you see?"*; let them propose the one line that would have caught it. Four of the six bugs below are **silent**: the run completes and the numbers look plausible. The habit to teach is the **check**, not the fix: step 0 equals the base; the trainable count; `base` is a different object; `optimizer` sees the new parameters; the base weight did not move.

### Bug D1 — a patch that is not zero (SILENT)

```python
# DELIBERATE BUG D1 (SILENT): B starts random, not zero, so the "patch" changes the model before any training.
class BadInit(LoRALinear):
    def __init__(self, base, r=4, alpha=8):
        super().__init__(base, r, alpha)
        nn.init.normal_(self.B, std=0.02)                 # the bug: should be nn.init.zeros_(self.B)


bad = copy.deepcopy(base)
bad.enc.requires_grad_(False)
for blk in bad.enc.blocks:
    blk.q = BadInit(blk.q)
    blk.v = BadInit(blk.v)
with torch.no_grad():
    print("step 0 gap, bad patch:", round((bad(X_eval) - base(X_eval)).abs().max().item(), 4))
rbad = score(predict(bad), "bad patch, before any training")
print(f"{rbad['name']}: {rbad['correct']}/30   (the base scored {rb['correct']}/30)")
```
```text
step 0 gap, bad patch: 0.027
bad patch, before any training: 18/30   (the base scored 18/30)
```

*The `B` initialisation is random instead of zero.* The gap at step 0 is `0.027`, not `0.0`, and here the score happened to stay `18/30`, which is the danger: the model is no longer the base model and nothing announces it. Catch: the step-0 check, with `0.0` as the required answer (not "small"). Why it matters: every before/after claim assumes the "before" is the thing you say it is.

### Bug D2 — a parameter list made too early (SILENT)

```python
# DELIBERATE BUG D2 (SILENT): the list of parameters is collected BEFORE the patch is added, so the patch never trains.
def fit_too_early(model, steps, lr, seed):
    params = [p for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad]      # looked at the model too soon
    model = add_lora(model)                                          # new A and B appear AFTER the list was made
    opt = torch.optim.AdamW(params, lr=lr)
    torch.manual_seed(seed)
    model.train()
    for _ in range(steps):
        pick = torch.randperm(len(X_train))[:16]
        loss = F.cross_entropy(model(X_train[pick]), Y_train[pick])
        opt.zero_grad()
        loss.backward()
        opt.step()
    return model, loss.item()


stuck, stuck_loss = fit_too_early(copy.deepcopy(base), 100, 3e-3, 100)
print("biggest |B| after 100 steps:", stuck.enc.blocks[0].q.B.abs().max().item(),
      "  (the honest run reached", round(lora.enc.blocks[0].q.B.abs().max().item(), 4), ")")
print("final loss", round(stuck_loss, 3), " honest LoRA loss", round(lora_loss, 3), " base loss", round(base_loss, 3))
```
```text
biggest |B| after 100 steps: 0.0   (the honest run reached 0.1938 )
final loss 0.302  honest LoRA loss 0.005  base loss 0.52
```

*The optimiser was given the parameters collected before the patch existed.* The patch never moves (`biggest |B| 0.0`), the head keeps training and even lowers the loss (`0.302`), and the model is "LoRA" in name only. ## ❓ Questions Students Ask This Week

- **"Is the base model real?"** It's real as code and real as a model; it is tiny, and it is trained on template sentences. Say: *"same steps a real one takes; much smaller numbers."*
- **"Why doesn't the fine-tuned model beat the rules?"** 64 tickets and a toy encoder. Real teams compare against the rules first (Week 30) for this reason. The rules also had their 5 misses; the model has other ones.
- **"Why do the seeds disagree?"** The new head starts from different numbers and the 16-ticket batches come in different orders. With 64 tickets that is a lot of difference. *"The seed is a small experiment; the spread is the result."*
- **"Can I make `out_of_scope` better by adding more training rows?"** In the module's own offline run, adding balanced rows fixed one category and cost two others. You can try it as a stretch. Expect to re-measure, on more than one seed.
- **"Why does the training loss go to `0.005` and the score stays `0.633`?"** The model memorised 64 tickets (Week 5). The loss is on the tickets it saw; the score is on 30 it did not.
- **"Would LoRA be worth it for us?"** At this size no: `0.21 s` against `0.24 s`. At a billion parameters the trainable count and the optimiser's memory matter (K1 Q5 does the arithmetic). We did not measure that here.
- **"Does the patch change the original weights?"** No; `torch.equal(...)` prints `True`.

## ⚠️ Where This Lesson Goes Wrong

1. **The student reads `19 > 18` as a win.** The cure is the question *"how many tickets?"* asked at every table.
2. **The student tunes until billing recovers.** Stop it. The eval is frozen (Week 30). If they want to try a different `lr`, it goes in the workbook stretch *with the result reported either way*, and any conclusion is on more than one seed.
3. **Pretraining runs long on a slow laptop and the hook dies.** Start P5 at the top of the lesson and have the Hook running on paper during it.
4. **A student types `LoRALinear` with `x @ self.A @ self.B`.** That is D3, and the traceback is good teaching. Let it happen.
5. **A student forgets `copy.deepcopy`.** D4. It produces a table of zeros that looks like a result. Ask *"did any category change at all after 100 steps of training?"*.
6. **The teacher says "LoRA is as good as full fine-tuning".** Measured here: a mean of `18.5` against `19.0` tickets over six seeds, inside the noise. That is "not distinguishable", which is different.
7. **The teacher says "the regression was in the small class".** It was billing (14 training tickets). Say what was measured.
8. **The pretraining text is called "data".** It is a stand-in generator. The scan is real.

### K5 — both zero

```python
# k5_bothzero.py - Week 31 key: why A is random. If A and B both start at zero, neither can ever move.
class BothZero(LoRALinear):
    def __init__(self, base, r=4, alpha=8):
        super().__init__(base, r, alpha)
        nn.init.zeros_(self.A)                             # the bug: A is zero as well


z = copy.deepcopy(base)
z.enc.requires_grad_(False)
for blk in z.enc.blocks:
    blk.q = BothZero(blk.q)
    blk.v = BothZero(blk.v)
fit(z, steps=100, lr=3e-3, seed=100)
print("biggest |A|, biggest |B| after 100 steps:", z.enc.blocks[0].q.A.abs().max().item(), z.enc.blocks[0].q.B.abs().max().item())
```
```text
biggest |A|, biggest |B| after 100 steps: 0.0 0.0
```

Neither moves: the gradient of `A` is multiplied by `B` and the gradient of `B` by `A`.
