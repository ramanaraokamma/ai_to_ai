# 🚦 START HERE — Your Day One

**AI Academy takes 2–3 years and ends with you building real AI systems.**
Today is not about all of that. Today is about 30 minutes.

Read this page. Pick your track. Do the three things at the bottom. Then close the laptop.

> **Which should you choose?** If there is *any* adult who will sit with the learner for an hour a

**They are not a fork in the road.** Same concepts, same order, same vocabulary. The 36-week course was
built *from* the modules, so you can switch tracks or use the modules as the reference text alongside the
weekly classes.

> ⚠️ **Honest note:** the 36-week taught edition exists for **Levels 1 and 2** — two full school
> years. Levels 3 and 4 are complete as self-study modules and have not been expanded into weekly
> classes yet. When Level 2 ends (~2 years in), Level 3 continues on the module track, with the
> [teacher guide](teacher-guide/README.md) for adult support.

## 🔁 The weekly rhythm — Taught Course

```
  NIGHT BEFORE   15-20 min   Teacher   Read "What YOU Need to Know First" + Prep Checklist.
                                       Do the worked example yourself, cold.
  THE CLASS      60-75 min   Both      Hook (8) → Concept (18) → Worked Example (14)
                                       → Activity (20) → Wrap & Assign (10)
  AFTER CLASS    20 min      Student   Read the student guide chapter. Nothing rests on memory.
  HOMEWORK       45-60 min   Student   The workbook. Self-marked from the answer key.
  BEFORE NEXT    5 min       Teacher   Skim the answers + next week's prep note.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
       one class a week  →  36 weeks  →  a full school year of Level 1
```

**Four terms, nine weeks each:**

| Term | Weeks | Name | The big question it answers |
|:--:|:--:|---|---|
| 1 | 1–9 | Spotting AI in the Wild | What is AI really, and what is it made of? |
| 2 | 10–18 | Teaching a Machine to Guess | How do you turn the world into examples a machine can learn from? |
| 3 | 19–27 | Trust, Proof, and Pixels | How do you prove a model is any good — and what is it looking at? |
| 4 | 28–36 | Words, Fairness, and Your Own AI | How do machines handle language, who does AI let down, and can you build one and defend it honestly? |

Weeks 34–36 are the capstone: **The AI Fair Booth**, ending in Showcase Day.

# 🎒 Track B — Self-Study Modules

One module per week, **four sittings, not one marathon.** Marathons feel productive and teach nothing.

```
  MON  ~45 min   🪝 Hook + 🧠 Concept      Read slowly. Say each idea out loud in your own words.
  WED  ~45 min   🔍 Worked Example        Type the code yourself. Never copy-paste on the first pass.
  FRI  ~45 min   ✍️ Practice (6 exercises) 2 warm-ups, 2 builds, 2 stretches. Struggle before peeking.
  SAT  ~60 min   🛠️ Mini-Project          Build it. Then write 3 lines in your notebook about it.
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
       ~3.5 hours a week  →  one module a week  →  a level every 12-28 weeks
```

## ⚡ The 5-minute setup for Level 1 (both tracks)

Level 1 requires **zero installation**. No Python. No downloads. Nothing to break.

```
□  Bookmark these three sites (free, no payment ever):
      teachablemachine.withgoogle.com   ← you'll train your first model here
      scratch.mit.edu                   ← create a free account so projects save
      quickdraw.withgoogle.com          ← play with it for 3 minutes, it's the point

□  Make one folder on your computer called  ai-academy
      inside it, make a folder called  notes

□  Grab a physical notebook. A real one. Paper.
      Write "AI ACADEMY" on the cover and today's date on page 1.
```

That's the whole setup. **Five minutes.** (Level 2 is where you install Python — that's a school year
away, and the [README](README.md#-required-setup-per-level) has the exact commands when you get there.)

Also collect the **Level 1 kit**: index cards, graph paper, coloured pens, poster paper, one envelope.
Total cost: about a pizza. 23 of the 36 weeks need no technology at all.

> 🔒 **Before a child uses an online AI tool:** read the safety and privacy rules in
> [`teacher-guide/00-orientation.md`](levels/level-1-explorer/36-week-course/teacher-guide/00-orientation.md).
> Short version — Teachable Machine trains in the browser and nothing has to be uploaded, and you never
> need to give any site a real name, face, or email.

## 🧠 Three rules that make either track work

1. **The 20-minute rule.** Stuck? Struggle for a full 20 minutes before opening the ✅ Answer Key.
   The struggle *is* the learning. Reading a solution feels like learning and usually isn't.
2. **Busy week? Skip the stretch questions — never the project.** The projects are the course.
   A skipped project is a skipped week.
3. **Three lines in the notebook, every week.** What I built · what broke · what I'd tell myself
   last week. By Level 4 that notebook is worth more to you than any certificate.

**Missed a week?** Nothing is broken. Start the same week again from the Hook. This course has no
deadlines and no forward references — every week only needs the ones before it.

## 💬 One last thing

You will hit a week that makes you feel stupid. Probably Level 2 Module 2, or Level 3 Module 5, or the
day the transformer doesn't click. **That feeling is not a signal to stop — it's the signal that you've
reached the edge of what you knew.** Everyone who can build AI systems went through exactly that week.
They just went back, re-read one section, and did the project anyway.

You need curiosity and stubbornness. You already have the curiosity, or you wouldn't be reading this.

**Go open that one file. 🚀**
