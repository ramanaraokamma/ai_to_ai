The problem

A generation is being handed AI with no instruction manual

Children now use systems that make consequential guesses about them — and are told nothing about how those guesses are produced. The result is a specific, observable failure mode:

  • They treat a confident answer as a correct one
  • They have no vocabulary for "this was trained on data that didn't include you"
  • They cannot tell a demo that works from a system that works

The gap isn't awareness — awareness is saturated. The gap is mechanism. Almost nobody under 18 can explain what a model actually does.

What schools try, and why it stalls: a one-off assembly, a coding club that teaches syntax and never reaches learning, or a tool tutorial that expires when the tool updates.

All three fail for the same reason — they need a teacher who can answer "but why does it get that wrong?", and that teacher usually isn't in the building.

Don't over-claim the crisis. The defensible claim is the mechanism gap, not "kids are in danger". Stay on what you can evidence.

Proof

One week, as evidence of method

Year 3, week 12 — "How steep is the hill right here?"

The hour

  • A curve on squared paper; pick one point
  • Measure how far up, how far along, over a tiny step — divide
  • Repeat elsewhere; notice the number changes
  • Notice the sign says which way is downhill

No symbols. No rules to memorise. A division they performed themselves.

That division is how every AI model learns: find which way is downhill, step, repeat. The mechanism arrives before the vocabulary — and arrives as arithmetic a 14-year-old can check.

The word gradient appears weeks later, as a label for something already familiar. That sequencing is what the pacing ladder exists to guarantee.

This is the "show, don't tell" slide. It demonstrates pedagogical craft, which is what a specialist edtech investor is actually evaluating.

The product surface

Shipping today: print, or a private site

Already working

  • A static site generator that renders all three years into one browsable site in about three seconds
  • Two access tiers — student and teacher — where teacher content is encrypted at build time (AES-256-GCM). The answers are not merely hidden in the interface; they are not present in readable form
  • Runs offline, bound to localhost. No cloud dependency, no per-seat infrastructure cost
  • Print-first — every page prints cleanly, answer keys separable

Why the encryption matters commercially: the same mechanism that stops a student reading the answer key is the mechanism that would gate paid content. The licensing primitive already exists.

Not built: accounts, cohorts, a teacher dashboard, progress reporting to a school, payments. Deliberately — those follow a pilot, not precede it.

Frame the missing LMS as sequencing, not absence. Building dashboards before you know how a real cohort behaves is the classic edtech waste.

The honest part

What is missing, and what could go wrong

Missing today

  • No pilot, no users, no revenue. Zero evidence any child has learned anything from this
  • Year 4 is unbuilt — and its subject matter (LLMs, agents) needs API access, so it requires a design rethink, not just authoring time
  • No accreditation or standards mapping
  • No accounts, cohorts, dashboards or payments
  • Single-author content — no independent pedagogical review, no classroom-tested revision cycle

Principal risks

  • Delivery risk. "20 minutes of prep" is a design target that has never been tested on a real, tired adult on a Tuesday night
  • Obsolescence. Years 1–3 teach durable fundamentals, which ages well. Year 4 would age fast and need maintenance
  • Distribution. School sales cycles are long and this has no reference customer
  • Commoditisation. A well-funded incumbent could fund three years of authoring — though not cheaply, and not quickly

Stating these is not hedging. The single most valuable next dollar goes to a pilot, because delivery risk is the one that cannot be reasoned about — only measured.

Do not soften this slide. An investor who discovers these themselves discounts everything else you said. One who hears it from you trusts the rest.
